# Why this challenge is legit — the trust block

Skeptical researchers assume an unknown solo "come break my thing" challenge is a waste of
time, a scam, or a rigged game where the author dismisses every finding. The antidote is not
louder claims — it's radical honesty and verifiable referents. Scams oversell; this
undersells, on purpose. Lead public posts and cold-mail with a short version of the block
below; it reads as legitimate precisely because it concedes so much.

## The full block (adapt length to the venue)

> **Why trust this.** We're not going to oversell it — that's the point.
>
> - **It hasn't been externally validated, and we say so.** Every test and review to date is
>   ours. Internal evidence is not validation. This challenge exists *because* that gap is
>   real — a confirmed break would be the first outside result, not a re-confirmation.
> - **The tool adjudicates, not the author.** A read-only inspector reads your token the same
>   way the target does and rules on whether you broke it. If it says invalid and the target
>   acted anyway, that gap is the finding — settled before we even reply.
> - **The claim is falsifiable and narrow.** One line, one pass/fail: make the target act (or
>   the sidecar say ALLOW) without a valid, current, correctly-bound, single-use, signed
>   token. We publish the boundaries we *don't* defend (DoS, key theft, non-adopting targets)
>   so you don't waste time and we can't move the goalposts.
> - **It's on the public record.** Formally specified (canon v0.9.8.4), open source (AGPL),
>   a citable Zenodo DOI, a named author with an ORCID, and a public verification ledger that
>   records corrections and retractions, not just wins.
> - **Good-faith testing is authorized.** A written Authorization-to-Test covers the named
>   hosts and window; a clean run that finds nothing is recorded as exactly that — never as
>   "unbreakable."

## The one-liner (for tight venues — X, HN title-adjacent, a reply)

> Honest version: nobody outside the project has broken it *or tested it* — this challenge is
> how we find out. A tool decides if you win, the claim is one falsifiable line, and it's all
> on the public record (AGPL + a DOI + a ledger that logs our own corrections).

## Why this works (the reasoning, for you)

Every other item in the recruiting kit lowers *friction*; this one lowers *suspicion*, which
for skilled, stake-free researchers is the bigger barrier. The move is counterintuitive:
admitting the system is unproven makes people *more* likely to engage, because it signals you
want a real result rather than a marketing testimonial — and a real result is the only thing
that gets G5 across the door.
