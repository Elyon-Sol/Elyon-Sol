Same target, three different motivations. No-cash recognition filters out bounty hunters but
not these pools — each is motivated by something a bounty can't buy. Match the pitch to the
door. Every one routes to the 60-second quickstart and security@elyon-sol.io; all keep the
honest-scope line and never show internal reviews.

## A. University security clubs & CTF teams (motivation: the puzzle + a résumé line)

> **Subject: a live target for your next meeting — break a signed-capability gate**
>
> Looking for a fresh, novel target for your club or team? We run a live admission gate and
> we're daring people to break it. It's not a web-app CTF — it's a custom cryptographic
> protocol: every approved action carries a one-time, signed token bound to that exact
> action; the target refuses anything else. First team/person to make it act on a call it
> should refuse takes permanent first-blood on our public wall of fame.
>
> Why it's a good club night: you can throw real attacks in ~60 seconds (no signup), the
> scope is crisp and safe (four hosts, real authorization on file), and there's a tool that
> tells you if you actually broke it. Rewards are résumé-grade: a named entry in a public
> verification ledger, a CVE where it applies, co-credit on a DOI-tied academic record, and
> a written reference from the author. Quickstart: [SITE URL]/#redteam. Happy to do a
> 20-minute intro call for the group.

## B. AI-safety / AI-security researchers (motivation: mission)

> **Subject: help pressure-test a verifiable oversight layer for AI actions**
>
> If an AI agent tries to act, what actually stops the unauthorized action *before* it
> happens? Elyon-Sol is one concrete answer: a fail-closed gate that refuses any
> machine-initiated action lacking a valid, signed, single-use permission bound to exactly
> that action — and a target that re-verifies before doing anything. Formally specified,
> open (AGPL), live over real TLS.
>
> Honest status: it holds against every attack we've run in-house, but **no external party
> has tested it** — and internal evidence isn't validation. That's the ask. If you care
> about verifiable AI oversight, come try to break the guarantee: [SITE URL]/#redteam (first
> attacks in ~60 seconds). A confirmed break is credited permanently and by name, with
> Zenodo co-credit and fix co-authorship — the point is a public good with your name on it.
> Know someone who'd be the right adversary? A name helps as much as an attempt.

## C. Early-career security researchers (motivation: portfolio + firsts)

> **Subject: get a CVE and a named credit — break a live admission gate**
>
> Building a security résumé? Here's a clean shot at real, citable credentials. We run a live
> admission gate with a specific, falsifiable claim, and the first person to break it gets:
> permanent first-blood on a public wall of fame, a named entry in the project's verification
> ledger, a CVE where applicable, co-credit on a Zenodo record tied to your ORCID, and a
> signed reference from the author describing exactly what you found.
>
> No gatekeeping to start: the surface is public, you can run your first attacks in about a
> minute (`curl` + `jq`), and a tool tells you if your break is real before you even send it.
> No cash — but for a first CVE and a citable finding, this beats a $200 bounty. Start:
> [SITE URL]/#redteam.
