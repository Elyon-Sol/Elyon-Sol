# Safe-harbor clause

> Not legal advice. This is the good-faith safe harbor for security research on the in-scope
> hosts, surfaced in `SECURITY.md` and on the site. Have counsel review the wording for your
> jurisdiction before relying on it; the counsel-reviewed version governs.

---

## Draft clause

We support good-faith security research and will not pursue or support legal action against
researchers for security testing and disclosure conducted in accordance with this program.

If you make a good-faith effort to comply with this policy during your research, we will:

- consider your testing of the in-scope systems to be **authorized** under the applicable
  computer-misuse and anti-hacking laws, and authorized with respect to any applicable
  anti-circumvention law, for the duration of the engagement window;
- waive any claim against you arising from accidental, good-faith violations of this policy;
  and
- work with you to understand and resolve the issue promptly, and not recommend or pursue
  legal action related to your research.

This authorization applies **only** to the systems explicitly listed as in scope, **only**
during the stated engagement window, and **only** to activity that complies with the program
rules of engagement. It does **not** authorize: testing of systems not in scope; denial-of-
service or availability attacks; social engineering; physical access; accessing, modifying,
exfiltrating, or destroying data beyond the minimum needed to demonstrate a finding; or
disclosing findings before the agreed coordinated-disclosure date.

You are expected to comply with all applicable laws. This safe harbor is a statement of the
Owner's intent and does not bind any third party (including the cloud provider, registrar, or
certificate authority), and does not waive any third party's rights. If legal action is
initiated by a third party against a researcher who complied with this policy, we will take
reasonable steps to make it known that the activity was authorized.

Authorization may be revoked at any time by written notice; upon revocation, testing must
cease immediately.
